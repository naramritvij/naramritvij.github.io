Anomaly Detection for Video Surveillance

commit 71dd3f0 · Keras CNN · OpenCV · Tkinter · SQLite

The problem

Reviewing surveillance footage for unusual events is almost entirely manual — someone either watches it live or scrubs through hours of recording after the fact, and most of what they watch is nothing happening.

What I built

A desktop anomaly-detection tool: a Keras CNN trained to distinguish normal from anomalous frames, OpenCV handling the video capture and preprocessing pipeline, a Tkinter interface for loading a feed and watching flags appear in real time, and a SQLite log recording every flagged event with a timestamp for later review.

Design decisions

  • Frame-level CNN over full video models — kept the model simple and fast enough to run near real time on ordinary hardware, trading some temporal context for responsiveness.
  • Logged, not just alerted — every flag is written to SQLite with a timestamp, so a reviewer can go back and audit what was flagged and why, rather than trusting a one-time alert.
  • Desktop-first — Tkinter was the right fit for a self-contained tool that needs no server and can run directly against a local or IP camera feed.
