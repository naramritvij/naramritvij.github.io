NeuralVerse

commit c27a48e · 20 chapters · interactive visualization

The problem

Deep learning concepts — gradients, backprop, attention — are usually taught through static slides or dense math, which makes the intuition behind them harder to hold onto than the formulas themselves.

What I built

NeuralVerse is a 20-chapter interactive walkthrough of deep learning, from a single perceptron up through transformer attention, where each chapter pairs a short explanation with something you can manipulate directly — nudging a weight, watching a loss surface shift, stepping through a forward pass node by node.

Ch. 1–6
perceptrons → backprop
Ch. 7–13
CNNs → RNNs
Ch. 14–20
attention → transformers

Design decisions

  • Interaction before explanation — each chapter leads with something to manipulate, then explains what just happened, rather than the reverse.
  • Progressive structure — chapters build strictly on prior ones, so the visualizer for attention assumes you've already built intuition for weighted sums and softmax a few chapters earlier.
  • Client-side only — every visualization runs in the browser with no backend, so it stays fast and free to host.

Why this mattered to me

Building the visualizations forced a more precise understanding of each concept than reading about it ever did — you can't animate backprop correctly without actually tracing the gradient flow yourself.
