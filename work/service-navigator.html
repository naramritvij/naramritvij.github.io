Service Navigator

A research-led newcomer settlement experience that turns fragmented SIN, OHIP, banking, housing, and tax tasks into guided workflows with preparation, warnings, progress cues, and clear next steps.

01 / overview

Design trust into high-stakes first-time tasks.

Newcomers often navigate unfamiliar terminology and disconnected services while worrying that a mistake may have legal or financial consequences. Service Navigator was designed to reduce that uncertainty by making each task sequential, explicit, and predictable.

0196% task completion

24 of 25 observed task attempts were completed in the moderated study.

02 / architecture

Information architecture organized around the newcomer journey.

Rather than mirroring the structure of government websites, the experience starts from the newcomer’s goals and routes each goal through the same preparation → guided action → confirmation pattern.

system_viewarchitecture / data flow

The system is consistent by design: each task can differ in content while preserving the same mental model for documents, warnings, progress, and completion.

06 / decisions

Product decisions supported by research

  • Tunnel, don’t sprawl - one clear next action per screen reduces decision load for first-time, anxious users.
  • Prepare before proceeding - documents and prerequisites appear before the user begins an application flow.
  • Warn early and consistently - risk and scam guidance uses a repeatable visual language rather than appearing as one-off notices.
  • Confirm and close - every task ends with a clear explanation of what happens next.
research lesson

AI-assisted evaluation helped structure data and find logical gaps, but moderated observation exposed the housing step-tracker failure immediately. The project reinforced that human perception cannot be inferred reliably from a logically correct flow.
